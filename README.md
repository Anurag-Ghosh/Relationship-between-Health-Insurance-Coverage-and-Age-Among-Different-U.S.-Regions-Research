#### Scenario: 
Assessed if there were statistically significant differences in the relationship between health insurance coverage and age among different U.S. regions.
#### Method:
My hypothesis (called alternative hypothesis in the research paper) is that there were statistically significant differences in the relationship between health insurance coverage among different U.S. regions. I gathered 2017 U.S. Census Bureau Health Insurance data and cleaned the data to get the relevant fields that I was looking to analyze. Calculated summary statistics (mean, standard error, and sample size) and graphed the uninsured rates among the Young (under 19 years), Middle-Aged (19-64 years), and Senior (65 years and over) age groups between the main 4 U.S. regions (Northeast, Midwest, South, and West). Since there were three age group tables that had to be analyzed, I had to conduct three separate hypothesis tests. For all three age groups, I used the same null and alternative hypothesis. The null hypothesis being that average uninsured rates across all the regions are the same. The alternative hypothesis being the that the average uninsured rates across all the regions are not the same. In order to test if there were any significant differences between the uninsured rates across the four regions among the three age groups, I would use the One Way ANOVA(Analysis of Variance) analysis on each of the age group tables to obtain the required Fcalc and Fcrit values to conduct the hypothesis test. For each of the One Way ANOVAs, I would reject the null hypothesis if Fcalc was greater than Fcrit. 

#### Quick Summary of Results
For the Young and Middle-Aged groups, since the Fcalc was greater than the Fcrit value, I would reject the null hypothesis that the average uninsured rates across all the regions are the same (differences in uninsured rates were statistically significant). However, since the Fcrit value was greater than the Fcalc value for the Senior age groups, I would fail to reject the null hypothesis. This means that there were not any statistically significant differences for the average uninsured rate between U.S. regions for the age group that was 65 years and older (this is likely due to people 65 years and older having access to MediCare regardless of income). Also worth noting is that between the 3 age groups, the Middle-Aged group had the highest uninsured rates. And between the 4 regions, the Southern region had the highest uninsured rates on average.
